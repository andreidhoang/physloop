**How the official score is built** (verified from Apache-2.0 code on the `e2e_challenge` branch, last commit 20235345, 2026-09-18)
- **Scene score.** Per rollout, the score is 0 on a hard failure, checked in order `collision_at_fault` → `offroad` → `left_corridor_laterally`. Otherwise it is min(progress_clipped_rel / 0.8, 1), and clips with GT travel < 5 m get full score. Source: `aggregation/scene_score.py`; `schema.py` (`progress_saturation_threshold: float = 0.8`, `min_gt_distance_for_full_score_m: float = 5.0`).
- **At-fault definition.** "At fault" is geometric: `collision_at_fault` = `(collision_front > 0) | (collision_lateral > 0)`, so rear collisions are never at fault (`aggregation/processing.py`).
- **The 4 m cap is scored as failure.** Config sets `max_dist_to_gt_trajectory: 4.0`. Timesteps after `dist_to_gt_trajectory >= 4.0` are removed (`aggregation/main.py`), and `left_corridor_laterally` (lateral distance ≥ the same 4.0 m) is a hard failure (`scorers/ground_truth.py`).
- **Aggregation and ranking.** Per-scene means over rollouts are fitted with Drive-IRT `BetaIrt` ("zoib"), 1000 epochs, seed 42, pinned to commit `82ddd5a4…`. Ranking uses the 95% rank-interval upper bound, ascending; ties break on `avg_dist_between_incidents_at_fault`, then point-estimate rank. The live board confirms rank ≠ PCS order: PCS 1386 ranks above 1424 because both intervals end at rank 55.
- **Minimum panel.** "six subjects minimum" for zoib on 441 scenes; below that the evaluator falls back to an arithmetic average with no rank interval.
- **Local references.** 8 subjects × 441 scenes × 3 rollouts (`alpamayo1`, `vavam-nonlinear`, `vavam-linear`, `alternative_1..5`; the alternatives' provenance is not recorded). Local scale anchors are `alternative_2` → 600 and `alpamayo1` → 2000 ("These numbers are an arbitrary local scale"). The contract is 10 Hz, "200 simulation steps and 1.7 s of force-GT".
- **Renderer variants.** [paraphrase, Space §02] The NRE Harmonizer diffusion post-processing is off for initial runs, on for PAI final runs, and off for nuPlan final runs. The EC2 config comment confirms the unharmonized renderer is used until the final reruns.

### Inferences
- **G1's "same 441 scenarios" is sound locally, but it is not the challenge's test set.** The only number independent of PhysLoop is the private-set PCS. That is limited to three official submissions per month shared across tracks, and closes on 2026-10-31. A ≥ 10-policy zoo cannot be scored there.
- **C5's rule that out-of-envelope rollouts are "never silently scored" conflicts with AlpaSim's scoring.** AlpaSim counts a ≥ 4 m lateral exit as a hard failure, so the official score mixes policy failure with validity exit. G2 must split `left_corridor_laterally` out before reporting failure rates.
- **The C7 taxonomy is partly simulator-native.** It overlaps `collision_at_fault`, `collision_rear`, `offroad`, `wrong_lane`, `min_distance_to_obstacle_m`, `plan_deviation` and `safety_monitor_triggered`. Close-encounter rate is an AlpaSim metric in the AR1 paper but has no scorer on the challenge branch. Stall, oscillation and red-light have no scorer (`scorers/*`); only a `traffic_light_status.py` map utility exists.
- **Single aggregate ranks are noisy at this scale.** By the challenge's own 95% rank intervals, PAI teams #2–#5 are statistically tied.

## Q2. Drive-IRT (model, items, abilities, intervals, external validation)

### Takeaway
Drive-IRT is a KE:SAI library: repo created 2026-03-18, **no license file**, 11 stars. It fits three IRT variants by stochastic variational inference:
- Rasch/1PL;
- 2PL;
- a zero-and-one-inflated Beta IRT (Molenaar 2022) for continuous scores in [0, 1].

Items are scenes; subjects (abilities) are policies or submissions. The challenge uses the **Beta IRT with per-item discrimination (2PL-type)**, plus per-item precision and two inflation thresholds. Its 95% intervals come from a **mean-field Gaussian variational posterior**, which the authors' own docs warn can under-report spread. There is **no external validation**: only in-sample goodness of fit and seed rank-spread checks. The docs concede that no held-out ground-truth ranking exists to arbitrate between model choices.

Extra facts (docs, paraphrased):
- Ch. 1 treats training-seed and bootstrapped route variance as separate from the posterior ranking uncertainty, so the 95% interval excludes simulator-seed test–retest and scene-sampling variance.
- Ch. 6 recommends checking Kendall's W across seeds and flagging values ≲ 0.8.

### Inferences
- **What the interval answers.** Drive-IRT's 95% interval answers "how sure is the ranking under this model, given these scenes and averaged rollouts?" It does not answer "would new scenes, seeds or a different renderer give the same ranking?" G1's test–retest and road-clustered bootstrap therefore complement it rather than duplicate it.
- **Ability is panel-relative.** Item difficulties are estimated from the other submissions, so adding or removing policies shifts every score (the docs' "moving baseline" drawback). PCS is a poor primary statistic for a paired B-vs-A release decision.

## Q3. Published evaluation numbers cited by the docs (nuPlan misalignment, pseudo-simulation, Bosch 2026, EUVS, Waymo approval guidelines)

### Takeaway
All five sources exist and most numbers are quoted correctly. Three qualifications matter:
- **(a) Pseudo-simulation's r = 0.89 is a sim-to-sim Pearson correlation.** It covers 83 *privileged* (ground-truth-perception) planners from 5 families, measured against **nuPlan reactive closed-loop simulation**, not real driving. Because the planners are privileged, the 3DGS rendering is never exercised.
- **(b) Bosch's ρ = −0.36 rests on n = 7, not 8, and does not reproduce from the paper's own Table 1.** Recomputation gives ρ = −0.14. Its ρ = 0.90 (n = 8) reproduces exactly. Both figures come from a meta-analysis of published numbers.
- **(c) The EUVS numbers check out against the ICCV primary,** not just the aggregator the doc cites. They are averages over 9 reconstruction methods; NuRec is not among them.

Waymo's 2505.09880 is a real approval-guidelines paper (Favaro et al., 2025).

## Q4. G3(a): has anyone correlated neural-reconstruction closed-loop scores with an external outcome?

### Takeaway
**Yes, partially, so the docs' "nothing exists" claims are wrong.**
- **Sim2Val (NVIDIA, CoRL 2025, peer-reviewed).** Pairs on-road drives of one end-to-end policy with closed-loop re-simulation in an internal neural-reconstruction simulator. NVIDIA's January 2026 blog identifies that simulator as AlpaSim. Per-episode Pearson ρ **exceeds 0.9** for two continuous kinematic metrics (n = 138 intersection and 75 curvy-road episodes).
- **OmniDreams (NVIDIA, 2026).** Shows the **policy ranking preserved when NuRec is swapped for a generative renderer**, for 4 policies, three of them camera-dropout degradations. No coefficient is given.

The real gap is narrower. Nobody has published a **multi-policy ranking or failure-rate validation of AlpaSim/NuRec scores** against on-road outcomes, or against an independent simulator, with a coefficient and n.

### Inferences
- **Independence hierarchy for AlpaSim/NuRec references**, least to most independent:
  1. Same rollouts re-aggregated (Drive-IRT).
  2. Held-out scenes, same simulator (challenge private set).
  3. Same scenes, different renderer (Harmonizer, OmniDreams).
  4. Different paradigm, overlapping policies (AlpaSim nuPlan/MTGS track vs NAVSIM).
  5. On-road outcomes (NVIDIA / Wayve / Waabi internal only).
- **Predictive validity is out of reach.** PhysLoop cannot reach level 5, so "predictive validity" in the psychometric sense (criterion = on-road outcome) is not attainable. The defensible headline is reliability plus convergent/construct validity (Q5, G3 section).
- **Why Sim2Val's ρ > 0.9 is plausible, and its limit.** It used paired episodes, continuous metrics and short (8 s) closed-loop horizons that keep the ego near the capture corridor. It says little about 20-s rollouts that leave the 4 m corridor, which is exactly where the challenge counts failures.

## Q5. G3(b): what measurement science prescribes for validating an instrument without ground truth

### Takeaway
Without a criterion, measurement science does not offer "predictive validity". It prescribes an *argument* built from several independent kinds of evidence:
- reliability (test–retest, inter-rater);
- convergent and discriminant relations with other methods (multitrait–multimethod);
- known-groups differences;
- responsiveness to controlled change.

Each is reported with its own interval. Robotics and MT-metric meta-evaluation have operational versions of all of these. Examples: sim-vs-real correlation with rank-violation metrics (Kadian SRCC, SIMPLER/PolaRiS MMRV); pairwise ranking accuracy instead of Pearson with few systems (WMT); perturbation tests in which a metric must prefer the unperturbed output (DEMETR, Perturbation CheckLists). A further option is combining a few gold labels with many proxy labels (control variates, prediction-powered inference).

**Caveats on precedents in 5.1–5.3 and Q4 row 4.15** (sub-agent, from primary text):
- **Kadian.** The tuned SRCC was selected by grid search over simulator parameters on the *same* 9 models (§V.B), so it is in-sample.
- **SIMPLER Bridge.** Pearson r is computed over n = 3 policies (Table V).
- **PolaRiS.** r ≈ 0.9 holds only after briefly co-finetuning the evaluated policies on ~350 simulated demonstrations (§5.1, §5.3); without co-training the correlation was too low to rank. The evaluator changed the object under test.
- **Lesson for PhysLoop.** Never tune the simulator or the deviation cap on the policies used to validate it. Re-score on held-out policies and scenes.

### Inferences
- **Mapping to PhysLoop:**
  - Test–retest across seeds → reliability.
  - NuRec vs Harmonizer/OmniDreams, and replay vs CAT-K → inter-rater reliability. Agreement is necessary but not sufficient: two renderers can share a bias.
  - AlpaSim nuPlan track vs NAVSIM → multitrait–multimethod convergent evidence (different method, same construct).
  - Open-loop minADE → discriminant evidence.
  - Degradation ladders → known-groups and responsiveness.
  - None of these is criterion (predictive) validity.
- **Statistic choice:** with n ≈ 10–30 policies, prefer rank-based agreement (Kendall τ, pairwise accuracy, MMRV) with CIs over Pearson r. SIMPLER documents Pearson's sensitivity to noise and non-linearity. A few extreme policies (e.g. a 1-camera variant) can inflate any correlation. Report within-family and between-family correlation separately.
- **Degradation ladders carry an assumption** that a degradation cannot improve true driving. Aggressive quantization or added latency almost certainly cannot. Camera dropout might occasionally reduce distraction, so this must be stated, and checked as a pre-registered prediction rather than assumed.

## Q6. Statistics checks (McNemar example, rule of three, Clopper–Pearson, clustering, multiplicity, non-inferiority, sequential)

### Takeaway
The arithmetic in C6/C8 is correct, but four statistical design claims need correcting.
1. **Clustering.** Exact McNemar assumes independent pairs, yet the same paragraph says scenarios cluster by road. Under clustering its p-value is too small. Use a cluster-adjusted McNemar or a log/road-level paired bootstrap.
2. **Clopper–Pearson wording.** "≤ 0.3% for 0/1,000" is the **one-sided** 95% bound (0.299%); the two-sided 95% upper limit is 0.368%.
3. **The kill rule is undecidable at n = 10.** An observed ρ = 0.8 has a 95% CI of about [0.24, 0.96]. Simulation: when the true ρ is 0.8, the rule kills wrongly 15.8% of the time; when the true ρ is 0.4, it passes wrongly 24.3% of the time.
4. **Stage 1a false-GO.** The criterion can be met only by abstaining. A 2% stratum (~9 scenarios) has ≤ 0.2% power.

Power context for G1: with 441 paired scenarios and 4% discordance, 80% power detects about a **2.7 percentage-point** difference. The doc's own example (12 vs 6) has power **0.22**.

  - Benjamini & Hochberg 1995, JRSS-B 57:289–300, doi:10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x: FDR step-up for independent tests.
  - Benjamini & Yekutieli 2001, Ann Stat 29:1165–1188, doi:10.1214/aos/1013699998: FDR under dependence.
  - Berger 1982, Technometrics 24:295–300, doi:10.1080/00401706.1982.10487790: intersection–union tests (title verified only). If GO requires every pre-specified stratum to pass non-inferiority, family-wise false-GO is ≤ α without adjustment, at the cost of power.
- **Non-inferiority.**
  - Blackwelder 1982, Control Clin Trials 3:345–353, doi:10.1016/0197-2456(82)90024-1: the null hypothesis is "worse by at least a margin".
  - ICH E9 (Step 4, 5 Feb 1998): the margin is pre-specified and justified; use a one-sided interval.
  - FDA Guidance "Non-Inferiority Clinical Trials to Establish Effectiveness" (Nov 2016; 81 FR 78605, public domain): the guidance addresses "how to choose the NI margin, and how to test the NI hypothesis".
  - Paired binary data: Nam 1997, Biometrics 53:1422–1430 (PMID 9423257); Tango 1998 score test and CI, which handle zero discordant cells.
  - Clustered matched pairs: Durkalski et al. 2003b, Stat Med 22:279–290, doi:10.1002/sim.1385.
- **Sequential testing.**
  - Wald 1945, doi:10.1214/aoms/1177731118: SPRT.
  - Pocock 1977, doi:10.1093/biomet/64.2.191, and O'Brien & Fleming 1979, doi:10.2307/2530245: group-sequential designs.
  - Lan & DeMets 1983, doi:10.1093/biomet/70.3.659: α-spending.
  - Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe & Sekhon 2021, Ann Stat 49:1055–1080, doi:10.1214/20-AOS1991: time-uniform confidence sequences.
  - Johari et al. 2017 (KDD; correct title "Peeking at A/B Tests: Why it matters, and what to do about it", doi:10.1145/3097983.3097992) and Johari et al. 2022, Oper Res 70:1806–1821, doi:10.1287/opre.2021.2135: always-valid p-values.
- **AV-evaluation practice.** One preprint uses paired tests between driving policies: Keswani & Bhattacharyya, arXiv 2512.17586v2 (Dec 2025), a Wilcoxon signed-rank test on 1,000 paired WOMD scenarios, with **no clustering**. No AV paper using a log- or road-level cluster/block bootstrap was found (3 searches). PhysLoop would be early in adopting it.

### Inferences
- **Methods prescription for G1:**
  - **Primary verdict:** per-stratum paired non-inferiority with a pre-registered margin. Use a Tango score CI, cluster-adjusted (Durkalski 2003b) or bootstrapped over roads. GO requires every pre-specified stratum to pass (intersection–union); INSUFFICIENT blocks GO.
  - **Regression flags** across strata: Holm FWER control. BH/BY only for exploratory screening.
  - **Zero-failure claims:** label the bound one-sided, and count logs/roads as n if independence is doubtful.
  - **Repeated gate runs** on successive checkpoints are a multiplicity issue. Genuinely sequential monitoring of one comparison should use confidence sequences.
- With 441 scenarios the gate can certify only margins of about **≥ 2.7 pp** (at 4% discordance). A smaller margin needs more scenarios (the 1,761 train scenes are leakage-separated from val, but using them for evaluation would forfeit train/val separation) or more rollouts per scene. More rollouts help only if discordance is driven by stochasticity, not by scenario content.
- **G3 correlation** must report CIs with the three-way rule and should target n ≥ 28–34 policies, or use the known-group pair design in the G3 section.

## Q7. Regulation and standards (NATM, UN ADS GTR, "UN R185", R157, R171, ISO/PAS 8800, ISO 21448, UL 4600, ISO 26262)

### Takeaway
The two claims most at risk of being fabricated are **substantially correct**:
- **ADS GTR and its companion UN Regulation** were adopted on **24 June 2026**, according to NHTSA in the Federal Register. The GTR's registry number is not confirmed in the UN document system; secondary sources say No. 26.
- **Draft UN Regulation No. [185] on ADS approval** was notified on 30 July 2026. It enters into force around **30 January 2027** unless more than one-fifth of Contracting Parties object. So "from Jan 2027" is right but conditional, and the number is still bracketed.

The docs nonetheless misuse these instruments in three ways:
1. **They are not industry release gates.** NATM, the GTR and R[185] are type-approval and validation frameworks. None mentions **paired regression against the previous release** or **staged rollout**. Their baseline is a "competent and careful human driver", not the previous software.
2. **ML requirements differ by instrument.** R171 (and R157) are technology-neutral with no ML-specific requirements. The ADS GTR/R[185] assume no online self-modifying learning. Only ISO/PAS 8800 is ML-focused. Mastery Plan P7's "what … UN R171 ask of ML validation" therefore has a mostly empty answer.
3. **Simulation credibility is regulated.** R157 Annex 4, R171 §8.2.1/Annex 5, NATM Annex III and ADS GTR §5.2.1 all require credibility assessment or correlation of virtual toolchains with physical tests.

NATM's Annex III defines the tool's validity **envelope**. That is the regulatory analogue of PhysLoop's G2 "validity envelope" and a citable anchor for it.

## Q8. How industry gates releases (Waymo, Aurora, Mobileye) and accuracy of the docs' summary

### Takeaway
The docs summarize industry practice as: "no single numeric gate; paired regression vs the previous release, stratified scenario groups, validity-checked simulation, staged rollout". Against the cited Waymo paper (2505.09880), the elements break down as follows:
- **Supported:**
  - No single gate, with a caveat: each criterion has its own targets.
  - Scenario groups graded with group-specific minimums.
  - Phased rollout.
- **Corrected:** release-vs-previous comparison exists only as one diagnostic input. Waymo's primary comparators are **human-derived benchmarks** (NIEON for collision avoidance; an adjusted human crash benchmark for predicted collision risk). "Paired" appears in no Waymo source.
- **Not in that paper:** "validity-checked simulation". It appears in Waymo's 2020 methodology paper and in the 2022 collision-avoidance-testing paper.

Industry-wide, "no single numeric gate" is **contradicted by Zoox (2026)**. Zoox's formal "safety clearance" gate requires one combined quantitative risk estimate (CIF) to meet targets before any safety-relevant release.

NATM and the UN instruments are type-approval and validation frameworks, not per-release industry gates. Listing them as "how the industry gates releases" conflates two different things.

## Q9. Failure taxonomies (NHTSA, Waymo crash typologies, simulator-native metrics)

### Takeaway
C7's seven categories (at-fault collision, rear collision, off-road, close-encounter, stall, oscillation, red-light) are not any external standard. Their match against external taxonomies varies:
- **Map well:** rear collision, off-road, red-light.
- **Map to NAVSIM only:** at-fault collision. NHTSA/SGO have no fault field; Waymo uses initiator/responder roles.
- **Partial:** close-encounter (TTC / near-miss) and stall (blocked / impeded progress).
- **No counterpart in any crash taxonomy:** oscillation (closest are comfort metrics).

C7 omits categories every external taxonomy has:
- vulnerable road users (pedestrian / cyclist / motorcycle);
- intersection geometry (left-turn-across-path, straight crossing);
- stop-sign violations;
- wrong-way driving;
- backing;
- severity tiers.

AlpaSim's native scorers on the challenge branch already supply at-fault (front/lateral), rear, off-road, wrong-lane, min-distance and safety-monitor triggers (Q1). **Close-encounter rate** is an AlpaSim metric in NVIDIA's AR1 paper but is **not** part of the challenge's scene score. The challenge scores only at-fault collision, off-road and lateral corridor exit ≥ 4 m.

### Inferences
- **Ground the C7 taxonomy in a published scheme.** Use NHTSA pre-crash numbers (#4, #6–8, #11–14, #22–31) as stratum and failure labels, plus Waymo's 11 crash-type groups for geometry. Severity cannot be measured in simulation beyond collision speed and partner type. AlpaSim currently treats all rear collisions as not at fault, which is coarser than Waymo's initiator/responder role.
- **Treat oscillation and stall as comfort or progress diagnostics,** not crash categories, and label them PhysLoop-defined.
- **Red-light needs new scorer work** (no AlpaSim scorer exists). Add a VRU stratum and a stop-sign category, which every external taxonomy has.

## G3 redesign evidence

### 1. Why G3 as written is not falsifiable
- **No reference is named.** 02 §2 G3 says "vs independent references"; 01 §4 Q3 implies Drive-IRT. Drive-IRT is fitted to the *same* AlpaSim rollouts (Q2 row 2.7), so ρ(gate, Drive-IRT) measures aggregation robustness, not validity.
- **"Predictive validity" is the wrong construct for the data available.** In the Cronbach–Meehl / Standards vocabulary (Q5), predictive (criterion) validity needs a criterion: here, on-road outcomes measured independently. Only NVIDIA (Sim2Val, internal), Wayve and Waabi hold paired on-road data (Q4). PhysLoop does not.
- **The kill threshold is undecidable at the planned n.** At n = 10 policies, an observed ρ = 0.8 has a 95% CI of about [0.24, 0.96]. The 95% upper bound falls below 0.6 only if observed ρ ≤ −0.05. Required n for a lower bound ≥ 0.6 at observed 0.8: about 28–34. Simulation: with a true ρ of 0.8 the rule wrongly kills 15.8% of the time at n = 10; with a true ρ of 0.4 it wrongly passes 24.3% of the time. See Q6 rows 6.9–6.10.
- **"First published predictive-validity number for AlpaSim" is not accurate as worded.** Sim2Val (CoRL 2025) already published per-episode ρ > 0.9 between an AlpaSim-class neural-reconstruction simulator and on-road logs for one policy (Q4 rows 4.1–4.3). OmniDreams published cross-renderer rank preservation for 4 policies (row 4.4). An accurate novelty claim: "first open, multi-policy reliability and convergent-validity study of AlpaSim closed-loop scores, with stated limits".

### 3. Recommended minimal defensible protocol (pre-register every prediction in `experiments/`)
1. **Reliability first** (R1). VIM conditions (Q5 row 5.27): seeds = repeatability; GPU/driver/AlpaSim-version drift = intermediate precision; second site or renderer = reproducibility. ≥ 10 policies × 441 `nurec_curated_val` × ≥ 3 seeds. Report the scenario-level seed discordance rate (the McNemar noise floor), policy-level ICC across seeds, and rank stability (Kendall's W; Drive-IRT docs flag W ≲ 0.8). If policy-level reliability is poor, stop: validity cannot exceed reliability. **Negative controls:** no-op changes (bit-identical re-export, equivalent execution path) must not be flagged. Their flag rate estimates the gate's false-block rate.
2. **Inter-rater agreement** (R2, R4, and R3 if accessible). The same policies and scenes with Harmonizer on/off and with replay vs CAT-K traffic. Report Kendall τ / pairwise rank agreement with CIs from a bootstrap over **roads/logs** (scenes cluster by road; Q6 row 6.5), and per-scenario agreement on failure labels (Cohen's κ), stratified by ego-deviation bin (< 1, 1–2, 2–4, ≥ 4 m).
3. **Known-groups / dose–response** (R7, R9). ≥ 3 degradation types × ≥ 4 levels. Pre-registered prediction: monotone decline. Statistic: fraction of the K known-ordered pairs that the gate orders correctly (pairwise accuracy with a Wilson CI), plus a trend test per ladder. This produces **dozens of ground-truth-ordered pairs** without on-road data. Report tie-aware pairwise accuracy (Deutsch et al. 2023), stratified by whether the expected difference is large (Kocmi et al. 2021). Screen outlier policies with a MAD rule (Mathur et al. 2020). It mirrors the "synthetic truth" logic of Stage 1a and MT-metric perturbation tests (DEMETR, Perturbation CheckLists).
4. **Convergent validity across paradigms** (R6, R9b). Target ≥ 28–34 checkpoints (Q6 row 6.9), NAVSIM-family on the AlpaSim nuPlan track vs NAVSIM EPDMS. Report Spearman and Kendall with Fisher-z (Bonett–Wright) CIs, pairwise accuracy, and MMRV (SIMPLER/PolaRiS). Analyse within-family and between-family correlation separately: variants of one family inflate r (pseudo-sim's 83 planners came from 5 families).
4b. **Hold-out discipline** (Kadian/PolaRiS caveats, Q5). Never tune the deviation cap, renderer settings or traffic model on the policies used to validate the gate. Never adapt policies to the evaluator. Re-score on held-out policies and scenes.
5. **Discriminant check** (R8). The gate should correlate weakly with open-loop minADE. A strong correlation would suggest the gate measures log-imitation rather than closed-loop competence.
6. **Held-out scene check** (R5). Submit ≤ 3 gate-ranked policies to the private set before 2026-10-31 (registration closes 2026-10-18). Report whether the order is preserved; no coefficient is possible at n ≤ 3.
7. **Replace the kill rule.** Kill or downgrade only on decidable evidence:
   - (a) policy-level reliability below a pre-set floor;
   - (b) known-group pairwise accuracy whose 95% upper bound is below a pre-set value (e.g. 0.75);
   - (c) cross-paradigm ρ handled by a pre-registered three-way rule: PASS if the one-sided 95% lower bound ≥ 0.6, KILL if the upper bound < 0.6, otherwise INSUFFICIENT. At n = 30: PASS needs observed ρ ≳ 0.77–0.78; KILL needs ≲ 0.35. At n = 10: PASS ≳ 0.87–0.89; KILL ≲ 0.05–0.07 (Q6 row 6.10).
   Otherwise report the interval.

### 4. Claims the protocol licenses vs does not
- **Licensed** (with n, CI, seeds, manifest):
  - "gate rankings are reliable across seeds (ICC …)";
  - "robust to renderer post-processing and traffic model (τ …)";
  - "responsive to controlled degradations (k/K known pairs ordered correctly)";
  - "converge with NAVSIM pseudo-simulation on n NAVSIM-family policies (ρ … [CI])";
  - "diverge from open-loop minADE as expected";
  - "order of ≤ 3 policies preserved on the challenge's private set".
- **Not licensed**:
  - "predictive validity" or "predicts on-road outcomes";
  - "validated against the real world";
  - any per-mile rate; "safe";
  - treating out-of-envelope (≥ 4 m) rollouts as policy failures without separate reporting;
  - generalizing from NAVSIM-family (privileged or nuPlan-scale) agreement to PAI-track VLA policies without the R1–R3 evidence on PAI.
- **Measurement-science basis** (verified in Q5): every step above maps to a recognized evidence source.
  - Kane 2013: validate the claims, not the instrument.
  - Messick 1995: an evidence portfolio across six aspects.
  - Campbell & Fiske 1959: MTMM convergent and discriminant evidence (R6, R8).
  - Shrout & Fleiss 1979 and Koo & Li 2016: ICC reliability (R1–R4).
  - Cohen 1960: κ.
  - Rutjes et al. 2007: no gold standard, so construct a composite reference or validate against outcomes. Agreement with a correlated-error reference is not validity.
  - Bland & Altman 1986: agreement, not correlation.
  - COSMIN 2010: responsiveness (R7).
  - Kadian et al. 2020, SIMPLER and PolaRiS: SRCC and MMRV practice.
  - Mathur et al. 2020: outliers inflate correlation.
  - Kocmi et al. 2021 and Deutsch et al. 2023: pairwise accuracy with ties.
  - DEMETR and Perturbation CheckLists: perturbation tests.
- **Regulatory anchor for G2/G3 vocabulary.** EU 2022/1426 Annex Part 4 §1.4 and NATM Annex III both make the credibility assessment *define the envelope* in which a virtual tool may be used. Both require correlation with physical tests, which PhysLoop cannot do. So PhysLoop may claim an internally evidenced envelope, never regulatory credibility (Q7 rows 7.1, 7.12).
- **Credibility level.** NASA-STD-7009B treats real-system data as the top referent. Without paired real outcomes, closed-loop outcome validity stays at the lowest validation levels (sub-agent reading, Q5 row 5.29). Component anchors (held-out real frames, logged trajectories) can be reported separately.
- **The bottom line in measurement-science terms.** Without a criterion (on-road outcomes), the strongest licensable conclusion is "a reliable, renderer- and traffic-robust instrument that responds correctly to known degradations and converges with independent simulation paradigms". That is construct-validity evidence. It is not predictive validity.
