## 1. Takeaways

1. **The strongest published latency → closed-loop driving result is Bench2Drive-Robust** (arXiv 2605.18059, 2026-05-18).
   - **Setup.** A fixed delay buffer on the *control command* in CARLA at 20 Hz, at 100, 200 and 500 ms, for 4 policies on 220 routes. No repeats, seeds or CIs are reported.
   - **Result.** The best clean model, SimLingo, falls from DS 85.94 to 28.45 at 100 ms (2 ticks). TCP-traj is unchanged at 100 ms and collapses at 200 ms. UniAD and VAD degrade gradually.
   - **What is still open.** No source found delays a *trajectory* that a tracking controller then follows from the current state. That is AlpaSim's `planner_delay_us`, followed by its MPC. **[C-30, C-31, C-34]**
2. **Robotics owns the mature latency literature, and it is crowded.**
   - RTC (NeurIPS 2025) is the common baseline. On the real robot (π0.5), synchronous inference degrades linearly with injected delay while RTC stays flat.
   - At least 10 follow-ups appeared from May to October 2026. PACT-VLA reports +15.0 pp over RTC at a 4-step Kinetix delay; FreeBar is accepted at CoRL 2026.
   - Driving has one asynchronous fast/slow paper, on CARLA: per-tick freshness raises route completion from 82.1 to 94.0. **[C-03 to C-08]**
3. **No closed-loop sweep of a reasoning-token cap on a fixed driving checkpoint was found.** The nearest evidence:
   - **TacitVLA** (AlpaSim endpoints; ledger).
   - **AdaThinkDrive** (NAVSIM PDMS, non-reactive): adaptive 90.3, always-think RL 88.9, never-think RL 88.3, with 14% less inference time than always-think.
   - **The reasoning–planning disconnect** (NeurIPS 2026): removing CoT changes planning only slightly.
   - **TRUST:** reasoning gains need steering before they reach the action.

   Positions stated by companies:
   - **Waymo:** the VLM is the slow path of a two-system design. No rate or latency is stated.
   - **Li Auto:** co-authors MindVLA-U1, where language steers the action diffusion through CFG with 2 steps.
   - **NVIDIA:** TacitVLA, with reasoning supervised in training but not decoded at inference.
   - **XPeng:** "language is a bottleneck" (ledger). **[C-10 to C-19]**
4. **Precision in closed loop.** Robot VLAs have many closed-loop LIBERO per-format results; W4A4 lands within about 1 pp of FP16, as in HoloQ-VLA.
   - **No driving VLA has a closed-loop per-format verdict.**
   - A 2026 SmolVLA study shows that a deployment-path change moved closed-loop success from 70% to 41% on one suite while another suite was unchanged. Its "INT8" and "FP16" ONNX artifacts were byte-identical FP32. **[C-24 to C-27]**
   - **Caveat on that study:** a sole-author, 6-page preprint on an RTX 2060, with one seed and 100 episodes per suite. The abstract does not significance-test the 70 → 41 drop; its p = 0.53 compares width-24 ONNX with PyTorch.
5. **Determinism.** Batch invariance is now LLM tooling, at a measured cost:
   - vLLM ships `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1` (beta); the only multimodal models it validates are Qwen3-VL-2B and 4B;
   - SGLang's `--enable-deterministic-inference` costs a 34.35% slowdown;
   - Thinking Machines measured 61.5%;
   - CoRun reports that batch-invariant kernels can add more than 2× latency.

   **No determinism or batch-invariance study for VLA flow experts in closed-loop simulation was found.** **[C-36 to C-45]**
6. **Few-step is a known speed lever with a published failure mode.**
   - Driving planners already run at 1–2 steps: MISTY, 1 step, 10.1 ms, nuPlan Test14-hard reactive 82.21; DiffusionDrive, 2 steps.
   - A one-step head on GR00T N1.7 cut action-head time from 45.3 to 5.0 ms, but LIBERO-Long success fell to 26%.
   - No driving paper found warm-starts the flow sampler from the previous tick's plan with closed-loop results. Robotics has the RTC family, SEAM and One-Step Flow Policy. **[C-28, C-29]**
7. **The already-ledgered papers are unchanged on arXiv.** FlashDrive, OPTED, RoaD, TacitVLA, ECO, "Perceives but Doesn't Brake" and VLA-Perf are all still v1 on 2026-10-28.
   - **FlashDrive now has public code:** `z-lab/flashdrive`, MIT licence, last push 2026-09-15. Its project page also links a Hugging Face weights collection and the label "CoRL 2026". So the optimized Alpamayo 1.5 pipeline, including its Jetson Thor 943.6 ms per tick (ledger), is a reproducible public baseline.
   - **TacitVLA's page shows "Code soon"** with no URL and no weights.
   - **"PhyAI"** is arXiv 2608.03682 v3 (2026-08-14): "Real-Time Physical AI at the Edge, Scalable Rollouts in the Cloud". It is a serving and batching systems paper (π0.5 and GR00T, Thor and datacenter); its numbers are in the 2026-10-03 ledger. **[C-01, C-02]**
8. **Closed-loop AlpaSim results not led by NVIDIA are appearing.**
   - LaPla: +33.34 pp AlpaSim success (abstract; affiliations not read).
   - TRUST: Somil Bansal's group (ledger).
   - ECO is Toronto-led with NVIDIA co-authors (Ivanovic, Chitta), so it is not independent of NVIDIA (ledger V18).
   - 2026 on-policy and closed-loop post-training methods use other simulators or other kinds of teachers: TerraTransfer++ on HUGSim, RoXDrive in a world model, RefineDrive on NAVSIM, RAD-2 on BEV-Warp, DriveMLP on nuPlan.
   - None does closed-loop correction of a VLA student by a VLA teacher at the states the student visits in AlpaSim (dated check). **[C-46 to C-53]**
9. **Reproducibility signals are evaluator-level, not model-level.** Examples:
   - Bench2Drive issue #203: in a TCP reproduction the vehicle falls through the map on the initial frame yet scores route completion 100;
   - AlpaSim #198: a scorer bug and a recalculated leaderboard (ledger);
   - Bosch's ρ = −0.36 does not reproduce from its own table (ledger: −0.14);
   - the mislabelled SmolVLA "INT8" artifact. **[C-54 to C-57, C-25]**

## 4. Raw opportunity and kill signals, unscored

**Opportunity signals** (what the literature leaves open; not ranked):
- **O1 — The latency → behaviour curve.**
  - The only driving latency-injection benchmark delays commands, at 3 levels, without CIs or repeats [C-30].
  - AlpaSim delays plans that MPC then tracks; its `planner_delay_us` is native and NVIDIA once ran Alpamayo at 200 ms [C-31, C-34].
  - No AlpaSim / NuRec latency curve and no paired-with-CI curve was found [C-35].
  - The finding that the best clean model is the most delay-sensitive has never been replicated [C-30i].
- **O2 — Tick quantization is checkable by construction.** At 10 Hz, 100 / 139 / 200 ms map to 1 / 2 / 2 ticks [C-34]. Sub-tick resolution depends on an unverified call-site question.
- **O3 — Precision verdicts.** No driving VLA has a closed-loop per-format verdict [C-26]. Robot-side precedents show that deployment paths change closed-loop success and that precision labels can be false [C-25].
- **O4 — Reasoning budget.** No closed-loop cap sweep on a fixed driving checkpoint exists [D11]. Evidence on whether reasoning matters conflicts [C-12, C-13, C-19].
- **O5 — Determinism.** Determinism and batch invariance for VLA flow experts in closed-loop simulation are unstudied [C-44]. LLM tooling exists and validates the Qwen3-VL-2B backbone family [C-38], and the cost numbers are published [C-37, C-39, C-40].
- **O6 — Warm starts.** No driving sampler warm-started from the previous tick with closed-loop results was found [C-29].
- **O7 — Asynchronous driving.** One CARLA paper exists; none on AlpaSim or NuRec [C-08].

**Kill signals** (what argues against a direction; not ranked):
- **K1 — Asynchronous / real-time chunking is crowded in robotics.** At least 25 papers from August to October 2026, including PACT-VLA (10-20) and FreeBar (CoRL 2026) [C-04, C-05, C-07].
- **K2 — Speculative decoding for VLAs is crowded,** and NVIDIA ships draft models for Alpamayo (PR #43) [C-09, C-10b].
- **K3 — Early exit, token caching and VLA quantization are crowded in robotics** [C-21, C-22, C-24].
- **K4 — Few-step is being raced.** NVIDIA deploys K = 5 (PR #45). Driving planners already run at 1–2 steps [C-28]. Edge may be few-step (P-018 note).
- **K5 — Generic latency robustness is taken.** A "latency-robustness benchmark for E2E driving" exists with a repo [C-30]. Only a narrower claim remains open: AlpaSim, the plan + MPC interface, paired with CIs, on a VLA student.
- **K6 — NVIDIA moves fast in every adjacent direction:** TacitVLA 10-13, "Perceives but Doesn't Brake" 10-20, ECO 09-25, AD-Memo 09-29, OPTED 09-17. The KV-reuse features already ship in TensorRT-Edge-LLM [C-20]. Edge's release is pending (ledger).
- **K7 — Determinism costs throughput.** Invariant kernels cost 34% to more than 100% on LLM workloads [C-37, C-39]. A batched, bitwise-invariant rollout engine could give back much of its batching gain [C-45, inferred].
- **K8 — Shadow mode stays open-loop.** No source found changes its evidential class. The correlation literature [C-46, C-47] keeps open-loop agreement from substituting for closed-loop verdicts.
- **K9 — Evaluator artifacts can inflate closed-loop scores** [C-54, C-56]. Any new curve needs its scorer pinned and audited.
- **K10 — FlashDrive's code and weights are public** (MIT; last push 2026-09-15) [C-01]. Alpamayo 1.5 serving work that does not change behaviour now has a reproducible public baseline to beat: 151.4 ms on an RTX PRO 6000 and 943.6 ms on Jetson Thor, 1 sample (ledger).
  - **Opportunity side of the same fact:** its bundled W4A8 + streaming + speculative + adaptive-step configuration can be re-run in closed loop for a per-lever paired verdict. Its own closed-loop table is not per-lever [C-26].
