## §0 One-page verdict

**#1 — "The simulator as a measured deployment instrument" (FOCUS path 1 as the research head; SCOPE 3, simulation-side inference throughput, as its floor).** Three layers, each worth shipping alone:
1. **Floor (branch-proof, no KC-L):** the AlpaSim evaluation driver made batched and bitwise. NVIDIA's RL path already batches Alpamayo across rollouts (AlpaGym `InferenceEngine`, `max_batch_size: 4`, `force_determinism: true` [AG-1, AG-2]); the evaluation driver runs eager at B = 1 [L7 4.4]. Port that batching with per-session generators (PR #26 [A-Q3.3]) and batch-invariant kernels. New claims: B = k bitwise identical to the reseeded B = 1 driver, and the measured invariance tax; net sweep saving 14–54 % [F7]. No such study exists for flow policies in closed loop [C-44].
2. **Instrument layer (the D3/I2 result, $0–28):** AlpaSim applies a delayed plan once per control tick, so 139, 171 and 200 ms are all simulated as 200 ms of staleness (3.0 m at 15 m/s) [E-§3.2; F1]; the first ⌈L/Δt⌉ closed-loop ticks apply ground truth [AS-1]; the MPC tracks from 1.0 s ahead, so staleness acts through stale inputs [E-§3.4]; stock AlpaSim cannot ask the 171-vs-100 ms question. A sub-tick `DelayBuffer` mode and a command-FIFO option go upstream (P-021), with a 10-tick positive control.
3. **Conditional headline:** the paired, stratified latency-cost curve, run only after the positive control registers, a pre-registered power criterion passes on stratum-targeted scenes from the 2,202-scene pool, and KC-L is answered.

**Why #1 beats #2 and #3.** #2, the deployment-lever verdicts at a fixed tick band ("what did NVIDIA trade for 10 Hz"), needs a checkpoint whose levers exist (KC-E), and every number is KC-L-gated. #3, the Jetson AGX Thor measurement artifact, needs a $5,499 purchase with an unverified lead time, has no like-for-like 2B baseline, and has no on-device line in JR2010863 (it reaches Q4 through its C++ driver; its device numbers wait for P-002). #1's floor is publishable now for its bitwise and timing results on synthetic inputs (its known-null verdict on NuRec scenes waits for KC-L), is the sweep engine for everything else (`02` S3), lands two contributions in NVIDIA's own repository, and its instrument findings count whether or not the curve moves. Scores, equal / weighted [F10]: #1 40 / 53.0; #2 34 / 44.5; #3 28 / 36.5.

## §3 The #1 deep dive

### 3.1 The incident, and why now

**The incident.** An OEM's OTA candidate is a 2B student. NVIDIA's own 2B students run at 171.2 ms median in FP8 on DRIVE AGX Thor (DRIVE OS 7.0.5, TensorRT 11.2, 6 candidates × 64 steps, K = 5 with fixed noise, greedy Domino at 8.30 accepted tokens per round, a 3,006-row prefill every request, the timer starting at token handoff; the README does not state the batch size) [A-Q2.2, Q2.4; F2-13], while Alpamayo 2 Edge is announced at 10 Hz for the same device with no camera, reasoning or precision configuration [A-Q7.1]. At Δt = 100 ms, 171 ms is 2 ticks late and AlpaSim would simulate it as 200 ms of plan staleness [F1]. Every AlpaSim run is lockstep by code: the runtime awaits `drive()` with no deadline, so wall-clock latency stalls simulation time and never enters the score [E-§3.5; AS-4]. Whether the published pre-2026-03-24 numbers (AR1's tables; the 1.5 card's 1.37 ± 0.10 on 913 scenarios) were produced with NVIDIA's legacy 200 ms planner delay is **unverified**: the CHANGELOG records only that the default became 0 and the legacy config was removed on 24.03.26, with no reason or cost stated [AS-5]; TacitVLA (10-13) replans every 100 ms at the default 0 [C-19]. So the release question "does the deployable model drive worse, and where" is answered today at a latency the car will not have, by an instrument that cannot yet ask the sub-tick question, on sweeps that cost 73–87.5 % of their GPU-hours in an eager, unbatched driver [L7 §1].

**Why now.** Edge's weights are due by 10-31; whether or not they ship, the floor and the instrument layer run on the released 1.5 [A-Q7.1]. AlpaSim contains the delay and external-driver mechanisms on both branches, so the instrument work is a config value plus CPU tests, not a fork [E-§3.1]. NVIDIA's own RL path already batches Alpamayo across rollouts, and the per-row generators it needs are an open NVIDIA PR [AG-1, A-Q3.3]; the cost of batch invariance is now published for LLM decode [C-36i, C-37, C-39]. The challenge closes 10-31 and the AlpaSim maintainer holds "simulation choices that … can affect the model inference" until the next release [AS-6]: S2 pins the protocol right after, and the week-1 issue asks two $0 questions, why the 200 ms default was dropped and which published numbers used it. The only verified latency-vs-closed-loop curves for driving are in CARLA, under command hold or a command FIFO, without CIs [N-1, C-30].

### 3.2 Hypotheses, mechanism and prediction slots

**Mechanism, from code.** The driver's plan is 64 waypoints stamped at absolute simulation times; the runtime stores it in the producing tick's rig frame and the controller re-anchors it with odometry at its first timestamp, so a stale plan stays fixed in the world [AS-7]; the MPC samples the plan at the current absolute times, clipped to its range, and its tracking cost starts at horizon index 10, i.e. 1.0 s ahead (`idx_start_penalty: 10`, `n_horizon: 20`, `dt_mpc: 0.1`; the challenge's nonlinear MPC inherits these) [AS-8]; a 6.4 s plan covers the 2.0 s horizon for any delay under 4.4 s. Staleness therefore acts through stale **inputs**: a plan computed d·Δt earlier reacts d·Δt later to whatever changed. Loss concentrates where the world changes inside the staleness window and the recorded margin is small: a stationary or slow lead in path (the margin shrinks by v·d·Δt, 2–3 m at 10–15 m/s and d = 2), a crossing or merging replayed actor whose gap closes within 0.2 s, and the handoff itself. Two caveats. Traffic on the branch S2 runs is log replay, so replayed actors never react; the only loss channels are collisions with replayed actors, corridor exits and progress [`02` §0.1 K9]. And a **ceiling effect** threatens the stationary-lead stratum: Alpamayo 1.5 drives into a stationary lead at 20.8 km/h with 19.95 m of clearance at zero delay [AS-9], and a Politecnico di Milano–led paper reports 58.1 % footprint intrusion [`02` S2]; a policy that already fails the stratum at d = 0 leaves little for d = 2 to subtract. The warm-up: the Alpamayo driver is not queried during the 1.7 s force-GT phase (`skip_driver_during_force_gt: true`), the buffer holds ground-truth references, so at d = 2 the first two closed-loop ticks apply GT15 and GT16 [AS-1, E-§3.2]; the handling rule is ★.

### 3.5 The 100 ms budget and ⌈L/Δt⌉, from AlpaSim code

L(n) = ViT + prefill + n·decode + K·expert + host. Under **serial decode** [F3]: with ViT cached and every stage at bound, L0 = 38.8 ms and the first tick boundary falls near n ≈ 13; with the measured proxies (newest-4 ViT, prefill proxy, expert at bound, host 10), L0 = 90.5 ms, L crosses 100 ms near n ≈ 1 and 200 ms near n ≈ 15, so n = 2…14 share one 2-tick band and 16 tokens cost 3 ticks; with all 16 frames encoded, L0 = 183 ms. Under NVIDIA's deployed **Domino** decode (16 drafted, 8.30 accepted per round, greedy [A-Q2.2]) the marginal CoC cost is of the order of 1.4–2.3 ms per token if a verify round costs 1.5–2.5× a serial token's weight read (an assumption) [F14], the same order as the expert step (≤ 1.7 ms), so n and K move the band comparably and the student's expert-headroom question is reopened. Which regime the student runs is a KC-E/T3 fact; both are carried. PR #45's 171 ms on DRIVE needs a 71 ms (41.6 %) cut to reach one tick [F3]. For the 10B teacher on Jetson the levers move whole ticks only between 8 and 9 (FlashRT `fast` 811 ms; no-CoC ≈ 478 ms, 5 ticks; K = 5 ≈ 748 ms, 8 ticks) [F4].

**Tick quantization, from code (all re-opened by the red team at `e2e_challenge e121e591`):** `planner_delay_us: 0` in the runtime config and the wizard default, and neither challenge preset overrides it [AS-10]; one `DelayBuffer` per rollout; its only consumer, `ControllerEvent._run_controller`, adds the current plan stamped `step_start_us` and reads `.at(step_start_us)` once per control tick, a `RecurringEvent` with `interval_us = control_timestep_us` [AS-2]; `item_at` pops while the next item's age ≥ delay and returns a whole plan, no interpolation [AS-3]; `pose_reporting_interval_us` never re-queries the buffer; Δt = 100 ms; `assert_zero_decision_delay: true` would fail at 50 ms control steps with 10 Hz cameras, so sub-tick resolution needs code [E-§3.2]. Applied staleness is ⌈L/Δt⌉·Δt ≥ L [F1]. Bench2Drive-Robust uses ⌊τ·SIM_RATE/1000⌋ at an inferred 20 Hz, so its "100 ms" is 100 ms applied while AlpaSim's "139 ms" is 200 ms applied: comparisons are made in applied-staleness seconds, never nominal milliseconds.

## §9 Risks, and what would change the verdict

- **The positive control does not register.** If 10 ticks of plan staleness leave the whole-set score within the replicate floor, the instrument cannot see staleness on replay traffic; that is a D3 finding about AlpaSim (controller and replay absorb sub-second staleness), reported as such; the curve is dropped, the floor and P-021 stand.
- **The pool cannot power the dynamic strata.** For Δ = 5 pp at σ_d 0.3 each stratum needs 163 scenes with 3 replicates [F13]; the pool's dynamic-actor counts are unknown until S2 labels them. Then the curve reports INSUFFICIENT per stratum with the MDE printed, and the headline is the whole-set step function.
- **The ceiling effect in the stationary-lead stratum.** Alpamayo 1.5 already fails stationary leads at zero delay [AS-9]; the stratum may show no additional loss. The crossing/merging stratum and the ★ third stratum carry the test.
- **NVIDIA publishes first.** It holds 200 ms-vs-0 ms internal data and withheld "simulation choices" until its next release [AS-5, AS-6]; TacitVLA and "Perceives but Doesn't Brake" show its authors on adjacent questions. The novelty wording is dated and narrow; the floor and the instrument findings keep value regardless.
- **KC-L.** Every closed-loop number is NuRec-derived; P-004 is unsent. The floor's bitwise and timing results, the CPU known answers and the latency axis on synthetic inputs are publishable now; the curves and lever verdicts wait.
- **The invariance tax exceeds ≈ 2×** (CoRun's regime [C-39]): the invariant arm loses its net saving; the non-invariant arm with the known-null verdict carries the floor.
- **AlpaGym's engine does not transplant.** It lives in the RL rollout worker on cosmos-rl; if its design does not fit the evaluation driver's gRPC worker thread, the port is a re-implementation and the Gap score falls further; the bitwise and tax claims are unchanged.
- **Config compatibility.** Whether `planner_delay_us > 0` runs with `assert_zero_decision_delay: true` on `e2e_challenge` is unverified until E1; the two checks act at different events and NVIDIA's 200 ms config ran, so the risk is low.
- **KC-E outcomes.** Edge not public: all arms on the teacher at 8–9 Jetson ticks, far from the deployment regime; Edge tacit: the n arm disappears; Edge not fine-tunable: S5(a) dies, nothing here depends on it.
- **P-002 timing.** No rental; stock and lead time unverified [D-F6.7–F6.8]; P-024 keys the order to KC-E in week 1; everything on Thor is otherwise "not measured on Thor".
- **Power and clocks on both sides.** 15 of 35 rented WS offers cap at 350–550 W [D-F6.2]; Thor's default mode is 88 % of MAXN with DVFS on [D-F1.12]. P-009 (iii), recorded in every manifest.
- **The instrument itself.** NuRec may vary run to run (#128, a hedged recollection); the evaluator changed mid-challenge (#198/#215); evaluator artifacts can inflate scores [C-54]. S2's noise floor and scorer test precede every arm.
- **Hours.** −104 to +10 h of slack; P-003 is unrecorded; P-011 may slip KC-1 by 0–3 days. P-029 names what goes.

**What would change the verdict.** Edge ships by 10-31 with weights, a decodable CoC and an evaluation licence, and a Thor arrives by mid-December: #3 rises (a model, a device, the 1–2-tick regime) and the student arms become the headline; the order stays 1 > 2 > 3. A FlashRT or NVIDIA release of a Jetson 2B runtime with per-stage, sustained and energy numbers: #3's gap closes to the C++ server and the HIL. An AlpaSim release that adds a trace-driven delay, an asynchronous driver mode or driver-side batching: P-021 or P-022 is withdrawn and consumed. A positive control that does not register: the curve is dropped and the floor is the whole capstone. P-005 at ≤ $2.5k: the floor and the instrument layer fit ($46–165); the curve and lever arms wait for the ≤ $6k tier; #3's device line never fits a cloud tier.
