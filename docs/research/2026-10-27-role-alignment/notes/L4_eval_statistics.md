- "Replicate" means one more rollout of the same policy on the same scene. Section 2 Q1 shows why "seed" is not yet the right word on the challenge branch.

**Bottom line for the report writer.**
1. **The gate's "seeds" are not seeds on the branch S2 will use.** On `e2e_challenge` (head e121e591, 2026-10-20) the Alpamayo driver stores the session seed and never uses it; sampling runs at temperature 0.6 / top-p 0.98 on an unseeded RNG. On `main`, per-tick reseeding exists but only with `force_determinism: true` (default false). NuRec itself varies slightly run to run. This changes what S2's "seed test–retest" and negative control measure, and decides whether common random numbers (CRN) are available at all (L4-1).
2. The biggest **GPU-hour levers** that keep every gate default are allocation, not exotic statistics: one replicate on more distinct scenes for verdicts on checkpoints PhysLoop did not train (L4-2, about −55% rollouts at equal precision under an illustrative ρ = 0.6), reuse of S2's teacher sweeps in S3 with a concurrent drift sentinel (L4-3, −54 to −136 GPU-h), and group-sequential looks over road batches with intersection–union curtailment (L4-5, expected n 0.84–0.86 × fixed when the student is truly equal).
3. **Anytime-valid methods cost width.** At one look, a Robbins normal-mixture confidence sequence is 1.55–1.85× the fixed-n half-width (against the two-sided and one-sided fixed-n bound respectively; ≈ 2.4–3.4× the sample). One-sided mixtures and betting sequences are somewhat tighter, but the conclusion holds. They belong only where looks are open-ended (S4 training-time monitoring), not in S3's one-shot verdict (L4-6).
4. **IRT subsets must not replace the scene set.** Published subset errors (≈ 1.2–3.2% of score) are the size of the gate's margins, and a deterministic high-discrimination subset changes the estimand. IRT may define strata or inclusion probabilities with Horvitz–Thompson weights (L4-8).
5. **Rare-event acceleration (NADE/D2RL/AST/CE) is not feasible in S1–S7.** AlpaSim's default traffic is log replay; the only perturbation handle (CAT-K) does not consume its seed; and pushing the ego off the log lands in the ≥ 4 m bin. What is feasible is covariate-driven replicate allocation for rare paired failures (L4-9).
6. **Rigor in the field is low and NVIDIA publishes no release statistics.** Bench2Drive reports single runs with no error bars; WOD-E2E is open-loop; the AlpaSim challenge ranks with variational intervals over unseeded replicates. NVIDIA's Halos SAFE technical brief (undated; first seen 2026-10-27) gives a credibility framework but no confidence levels, sample sizes or release rule.

**Inferences (labelled).**
- **Coupling mechanism.** With per-tick reseeding, two arms start every tick from the same RNG state. Coupling survives only while both consume the stream identically: same tokenizer, same number of CoC tokens before the expert draws its noise, same tensor shapes. Same-architecture pairs (BF16 vs FP8, flow-step ladders, latency injection on one model) should stay strongly coupled. A 2B student vs a 10B teacher, or TransFuser vs Alpamayo, should decouple from the first tick whose CoC length differs. *Inference, not measured.*
- **Horizon decay.** Once actions differ, states and rendered images differ, so even identical noise yields different futures. CRN's gain should decay over the 200 steps. *Inference.*
- **What S2's repeats measure.** On `e2e_challenge`, S2's "≥ 3 seeds" measure the full uncontrolled replicate noise (policy sampling, GPU non-determinism, NuRec variation). That is still a valid noise floor, but it is not "seed test–retest" and it cannot be replayed.
- Controller (MPC) and physics randomness: none was named in #128 or #149. Not verified in code.

### Q2. Sequential and adaptive designs

**Takeaway.** Group-sequential designs with O'Brien–Fleming-type spending fit the gate almost for free (n_max +2–3%) and save 14–16% expected rollouts when the student is truly equal. Anytime-valid methods have real 2025–2026 uptake in ML policy comparison, but their single-look width penalty is large.

## 4. First-principles rationale per finding

Every row keeps the gate's defaults: paired; cluster-adjusted by road; one-sided bounds labelled one-sided; INSUFFICIENT blocks GO; ≥ 4 m rollouts reported separately; margins and endpoints pre-registered. Where a method touches one of these, it is stated.

### L4-1 Seed semantics and CRN coupling audit (S2)
- **Mechanism.** Pairing by scene already cancels the scene's difficulty. What is left is σ_d² = σ_δ² + σ_wA² + σ_wB² − 2ρ_wσ_wAσ_wB: effect heterogeneity across scenes plus each arm's replicate noise. CRN raises ρ_w, the within-scene correlation of the two arms' replicate noise, by giving both arms the same random numbers. It never biases the paired estimate, because each arm's marginal distribution is unchanged (Law & Kelton's CRN argument). The gain is KA-B2: at ρ_w = 0.5 or 0.9, 41% or 74% fewer rollouts for equal precision (illustrative inputs).
- **Assumptions in AlpaSim.** (i) The randomness that matters is policy sampling (F1.5): log-replay traffic is deterministic, and CAT-K is off and unseeded. (ii) Per-tick reseeding (F1.1–F1.2) is the "synchronize streams" device, applied at tick granularity. (iii) Both arms draw from the RNG in the same order; this holds for same-architecture pairs and likely fails across architectures (inference, Q1).
- **Failure modes.** (a) Deterministic kernels may slow inference and can raise errors on ops without deterministic implementations (general PyTorch behaviour; inference). Reseed-only mode avoids that cost and still aligns the streams. (b) Porting the reseed into the pinned challenge driver changes the instrument, so it must be recorded, and a seeded sweep must agree in distribution with an unseeded one. (c) If analysts later treat seeds as independent across arms, the variance is wrong; pair by (scene, seed) and cluster by road. (d) NuRec run-to-run variation (F1.8) caps how identical a same-seed re-run can be.
- **Why it beats the alternatives.** It is free: the audit permutes seed labels on rollouts S2 already makes. It turns S2's "seed test–retest" into a statement whose meaning is known, and it gives a sharper negative control. Without it, every later pairing claim rests on an untested assumption.
- **Cost.** +5 h; 0 GPU-h for the audit. Deterministic-mode throughput is an S2 measurement (prediction slot).
- **JR mapping.** D3 (measuring the simulator as an instrument), I2 (simulation accuracy and reproducibility), X2, D4 (debuggability of runs).
- **Known answers.** KA-B1, KA-B2.

### L4-2 Rollouts to scenes, not replicates (S3, and S6 for untrained checkpoints)
- **Mechanism.** Two-stage sampling: Var(d̄) = σ_b²/n + σ_w²/(nm) = σ²[1 + (m − 1)ρ]/(nm). At a fixed rollout budget N = nm, m = 1 minimizes variance whenever ρ > 0 and scenes are available. With a per-scene setup cost c_s and per-rollout cost c_r, the optimum is m* = √[(c_s/c_r)(1 − ρ)/ρ] (KA-C). m > 1 pays only when scene setup is expensive and the within-scene share is large.
- **Assumptions.** (i) The inference target is a super-population of roads, which is what the road-level bootstrap implies. (ii) The 1,761 train-split scenes are exchangeable with the 441: the split was formed 80/20 over road groups. (iii) PhysLoop trains nothing on them in that stage. That holds for S3 (NVIDIA's Edge) and for per-format verdicts of Edge. It fails for S4 (trains on the 1,761) and S5 (on-policy correction), which keep the 441.
- **Key caveat for Huy (★).** If the claim is about *these* 441 scenes (a finite population), the between-scene term is not sampling error. Then only replicates reduce variance (Var = σ_w²/(441m)), and adding scenes changes the population. The plan's allowed-claims wording ("on n held-out scenes") is compatible with either reading. The choice must be made before S3's pre-registration.
- **Failure modes.** Contamination with `alpamayo_aligned` may differ between splits (the S2 report must cover both). The effective n is roads, not scenes, and the 1,761 may have more scenes per road (unknown, Δ6). Downloading more NuRec scenes takes time.
- **Why it beats the alternatives.** It is the largest "free" precision gain: same GPU-h, about 1.48× smaller SE at ρ = 0.6. It needs no new statistics, only the existing design-effect algebra from `03` §4.
- **Cost.** +2 h; −56 to −185 GPU-h at equal precision (KA-K), or 0 GPU-h for a tighter verdict.
- **JR mapping.** X2 (closed-loop evaluation design), D3, I2 (accuracy per GPU-hour).

### L4-3 Teacher-arm reuse with a concurrent drift sentinel (S3)
- **Mechanism.** Under a pinned protocol P, S2's teacher rollouts are draws from the same outcome distribution as new ones. Re-running them buys nothing except protection against protocol drift, and drift is a real risk: the 1.5 card moved 0.81 → 1.37 with unchanged weights. A concurrent mini-control turns the untestable "nothing changed" into a measured equivalence. Seeded, it re-runs the same (scene, seed) pairs, so differences should be near zero apart from NuRec variation, which makes a sensitive detector. Unseeded, it is a two-sample equivalence on the subsample, which is weaker.
- **Assumptions.** Identical manifest fields (SHA, resolved lockfile, renderer and Harmonizer setting, GPU edition, `n_sim_steps`, seed mode). Edge must run in the same AlpaSim build as S2. If Edge needs a newer driver adapter, the protocol changes and reuse is off.
- **Failure modes.** A small mini-control (the illustrative 20%) has limited power against small drifts, so the equivalence margin (★) decides what "no drift" means. Time-of-run effects (host change) are what the sentinel exists to catch.
- **Why it beats the alternatives.** It removes the largest line in S3's cost without changing the estimand. The alternative, an unchecked reuse, would be a silent confound.
- **Cost.** +1.5 h; −54 to −136 GPU-h, −$54 to −$218.
- **JR mapping.** X2, D4 (reliable, fast evaluation workflows), I2.

### L4-4 Covariate and multi-fidelity control variates: measure first (S2)
- **CUPED mechanism.** d̂_cv = d̄ − θ(x̄ − μ_x), variance factor 1 − ρ²(d, x) (KA-J). It is valid when x is fixed before the run and θ is cross-fitted (MLRATE, F6.5), with θ re-estimated inside every road-bootstrap replicate.
- **Why the expected yield is low here (first principles).** Pairing *is* a control variate: it subtracts the teacher's outcome with coefficient 1, which removes scene difficulty. A covariate such as an independent teacher replicate or reference-panel difficulty can then only predict the *heterogeneity of the effect*. Cov(d, x) = Cov(μ_B − μ_A, μ_A), which is non-zero only if the student's sensitivity to difficulty differs from the teacher's. Open-loop minADE is expected to carry ≈ 0 (Bosch n = 7; ledger 10-03 row 3.3). MLRATE's > 70% and Wayve's on-road gains come from *unpaired* units, where covariates explain the between-unit variance that AlpaSim pairing already cancels (inference).
- **Multi-fidelity mechanism.** Every 200-step rollout contains its own 50- or 100-step prefix. Run all scenes at T steps (cheap) and a pre-registered random subset at 200; the two-fidelity control-variate estimator is unbiased for the 200-step mean (Peherstorfer, Willcox & Gunzburger 2016). The saving is material only if ρ(d_T, d_200) ≥ ~0.8 (KA-I).
- **Defaults.** Within T steps, ≥ 4 m exits are flagged identically at both fidelities. Exits after T are seen only in the 200-step subset, which estimates them unbiasedly.
- **Failure modes.** Outcome-dependent covariates (anything measured during the same rollout) bias the estimate (R6). The truncated score may not be computable from logs (open question O7).
- **Cost.** +3 h, 0 GPU-h to decide.
- **JR mapping.** D3, X2.

### L4-5 Group-sequential paired verdict (S1 tables; S3–S5 use)
- **Mechanism.** Error spending distributes α over K looks. O'Brien–Fleming-type spending puts almost nothing early, so the final boundary is close to fixed-n (n_max +1.6–2.7%, KA-D), while a clear result stops early: expected n is 0.84–0.85 × fixed at the powered alternative for K = 3. A futility boundary adds a ≈ 32% expected saving under the margin null, at n_max +12%.
- **Fit with the gate's defaults.**
  - Each stratum gets its own group-sequential test at one-sided α.
  - The intersection–union rule keeps false-GO ≤ α: a false GO needs the inferior stratum's own sequential test to reject, which happens with probability ≤ α.
  - Strata that cross efficacy stop receiving rollouts (curtailment). A futility crossing in any stratum makes GO impossible, so the verdict ends as **INSUFFICIENT**. It is not NO-GO, which stays reserved for the regression flags.
  - One-sided boundaries are labelled one-sided. After early stopping, bounds use stage-wise ordering (Jennison & Turnbull), because naive CIs are biased.
- **Look unit under clustering.** Replicates within a scene are correlated, so "one more replicate" is not a third of the information. Looks come after **pre-registered random road batches, stratified** so each batch represents every stratum. The information fraction is observed information (inverse cluster-robust variance), handled by Lan–DeMets spending, which tolerates unequal increments. Use the wild cluster bootstrap-t when roads < 30 (ledger 10-03 row 6.5).
- **Failure modes.** Too few roads per batch makes variance estimates unstable. Batch-to-batch drift (host change) is the L4-3 sentinel's job. Over-running a planned look is handled by the spending function.
- **Evidence it transfers.** STEP saved up to 32% of robot-policy trials (F2.2); Netflix runs sequential regression detection on canaries (F2.3). Neither handles road clustering; that adaptation is ★.
- **Why S1.** The boundary and expected-n tables are pure CPU arithmetic and belong with the power analysis, which survives KC-1's "ship the power half alone".
- **Cost.** S1 +3 h, S3–S5 +4 h; savings KA-D/KA-K.
- **JR mapping.** X2, D3, I2 (evaluation compute performance).

### L4-6 Anytime-valid monitoring only where looks are open-ended (S4, S5)
- **Mechanism.** An e-process E_t satisfies E[E_τ] ≤ 1 under H0 for any stopping time τ. Ville's inequality then gives P(sup_t E_t ≥ 1/α) ≤ α, so one can look after every checkpoint and stop at will. Betting confidence sequences for bounded paired differences (Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024) adapt to the variance.
- **The price, made explicit (KA-E).** At one look, a Robbins normal-mixture sequence tuned for n is 1.549× the two-sided and 1.845× the one-sided fixed-n half-width (≈ 2.4–3.4× the sample). Because the mixture bounds both tails, the one-sided figure overstates the penalty somewhat; one-sided mixtures and betting sequences are tighter, and the conclusion holds either way. With a Beta(1,1) mixture, 12 vs 6 discordant pairs give E = 0.743. At a 2:1 split, E ≥ 20 needs 90 discordant pairs, where fixed-n reaches p ≤ 0.05 at 30. This agrees with F2.1, where 3 discordant of 120 gave at most 3.675. So S3's one-shot verdict should not use it.
- **Where it pays.** S4 training-time monitoring: the number of checkpoints at which one wants to look is not fixed. Stopping a futile reward arm early saves H100-hours (S4 trains 225–450 H100-h), and the S4 acceptance verdict on the 441 held-out stays a separate, fixed design.
- **Validity conditions.** Increments must be road-level (cluster) units to keep the martingale property; scene-level increments within one road are not independent (inference). Multiplying e-values across S5's ablation rounds is valid only if the rounds test the *same* null; they test different models, so each round needs its own e-process or a multiplicity rule (★).
- **Cost.** +3 h.
- **JR mapping.** D4 (monitoring training runs), Q3 (RL), X2.

### L4-7 Intersection–union power accounting and stratum-aware allocation
- **Mechanism.** GO requires every stratum to pass. For road-disjoint strata the overall GO power is the product of the per-stratum powers (KA-F): four strata at 80% each give 41%, and 80% overall needs 94.6% per stratum. This is the quantitative reason INSUFFICIENT will be common.
- **Allocation.** Neyman allocation minimizes *pooled* variance, but it starves the smallest stratum (KA-F: 23.8 pp MDE vs 6.8 pp). Under an intersection–union rule the binding stratum is the weakest, so the objective is minimax: equalize per-stratum SE relative to its margin. When a stratum has too few scenes, only replicates help, and they reduce within-scene variance only. The honest outcomes are then INSUFFICIENT, or pre-registered stratum merging (★).
- **Defaults.** Unchanged; this is accounting.
- **Cost.** +1 h, 0 GPU-h.
- **JR mapping.** X2, D3.

### L4-8 IRT as stratifier or inclusion probability, never a deterministic subset
- **Mechanism.** A deterministic subset S estimates the subset mean, not the 441-scene mean. The pre-registered margin then refers to a different quantity, with bias = mean_S − mean_all (KA-G: 0.100 vs 0.050). Horvitz–Thompson keeps the estimand for any inclusion probabilities π_i > 0, because E[Σ R_id_i/π_i]/N = mean d. When discordance concentrates in scenes flagged by *independent* data, the variance can drop sharply (KA-G: 14× in the toy).
- **Why LLM subset results do not transfer.** Their errors (tinyBenchmarks ≈ 2%, metabench RMSE 1.24%, Scales++ 2.9–3.2% MAE) are measured on one model's score across thousands of calibration models. PhysLoop needs a paired difference between near-identical checkpoints with 2.7–7 pp margins, calibrated on 8 policies. Scales++ names the core risk: selection assumes new models fail like old ones.
- **What IRT is good for here.** Defining difficulty strata, ordering or allocating replicates (L4-9), and diagnosing uninformative scenes, all with design weights.
- **Free check.** Fit on the 8 references, then test whether discrimination predicts the observed discordance between vavam-linear and vavam-nonlinear (same model, controller changed).
- **Ordering group-sequential looks by discrimination** was considered during this research and rejected (R4), because interim estimates would then target a discrimination-weighted estimand.
- **Cost.** +3 h, 0 GPU-h.
- **JR mapping.** D3, X2.

### L4-9 Covariate-driven replicate allocation for rare paired failures (S4)
- **Mechanism.** For a binary at-fault endpoint, scene i's replicate variance of the paired difference is σ_i² = p_Ai(1 − p_Ai) + p_Bi(1 − p_Bi), which is zero for scenes that never fail. Allocating replicates with m_i ∝ σ_i (Neyman across scenes) concentrates rollouts where discordance can occur (KA-H: −52.6% rollouts at equal precision on the illustrative mix).
- **Validity condition.** m_i must depend only on information independent of the current sweep: S2 replicates of the teacher, or the reference panel. Then each scene mean is unbiased and the estimate is unbiased.
- **Outcome-dependent version.** Adding replicates *because* a scene failed in phase 1 biases naive per-scene means (KA-H toy: E = 0.04 vs 0.10). The double-sampling estimator restores unbiasedness, but only if unflagged scenes keep a positive sampling probability, and it costs variance.
- **Fit with McNemar/Durkalski.** With unequal replicates, the scene-level paired difference becomes a proportion. The verdict uses the road-level bootstrap of the weighted estimator (★); the exact McNemar is no longer the right test.
- **Failure modes.** Misprediction: the KA-H case where "never-fail" scenes actually fail at 2% has 14.6% *more* variance than uniform. It stays unbiased; a floor of ≥ 1 replicate per scene and a cap limit the damage.
- **Cost.** +2 h; savings conditional (KA-K).
- **JR mapping.** X2, I2, D3.

### L4-10 Provisional noise inputs from the shipped reference panel (S1)
- **Mechanism.** The shipped references are real AlpaSim closed-loop outcomes on the same 441 scenes: 8 subjects × 3 replicates. They let S1 replace an *assumed* σ_d/ψ grid with *measured* provisional values at $0. They also give the first public estimate of unseeded replicate noise (ICC, design effect) on AlpaSim under the challenge contract.
- **Caveats.** Unseeded replicates; local runs used the unharmonized renderer (ledger 10-03 §Q1); Alpamayo 1 rather than 1.5; `alternative_n` provenance unknown; whether per-rollout records are present is unverified (F1.14). Values are provisional, labelled so, and kept internal until P-004.
- **Why it beats waiting for S2.** S1's power tables and KC-1's go/no-go on GPU spend are better informed. S2 still measures the real numbers.
- **Cost.** +3 h, 0 GPU-h.
- **JR mapping.** D3, X2.
